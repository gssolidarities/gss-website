TAKAYA.

Khartoum · 2023–2025 · 12 pages

One pot. One street. One country refusing to starve.

On 15 April 2023 the fighting between Sudan's SAF and RSF breaks open across Khartoum. The state apparatus for feeding anybody collapses within weeks. What stands in its place are the Emergency Response Rooms — decentralised, youth-led neighbourhood committees — and the takaya they run: communal kitchens cooked out of what the street can still find.

By mid-2024 the ERRs are serving hundreds of thousands of meals a day across the country. Across 2024–2025, famine is formally declared, cholera returns to Darfur, and foreign funding to the kitchens collapses. Many takaya are forced to close.

This is twelve pages inside one of the kitchens that stayed open. Characters are fictional; the events are documented.

For the volunteers of Sudan's Emergency Response Rooms — the neighbourhood committees who kept communal kitchens (takaya) open while the war closed everything else.
