THE UNWRITTENLEDGER

Caste in India has always been an accounting system. It counts births, counts marriages, counts who may draw water and who may not. It keeps immaculate books. This collection opens a second ledger beside the first — not to correct the state's arithmetic, but to record what its arithmetic was designed to lose. जे नोंदवले गेले नाही, ते घडले नाही असे नाही.

Debit — entered in the register

57,789 cases of crime against Scheduled Castes registered in India in 2023. 3,07,355 cases pending trial under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. 24 cases of untouchability registered nationwide under the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 — in a country where untouchability was abolished in 1950 and has not stopped.

Credit — refused by the register

The FIR that was never filed because the station house officer belonged to the dominant caste. The well a family stopped walking to. The teacher's hand that did not touch the copybook. The classroom seat at the back. Twenty-four is not a measurement of untouchability. It is a measurement of how untouchability is recorded.

Curatorial statement

WE ARE NOT
FILLING A GAP.

The standard justification for a Dalit archive is that the record is incomplete — that colonial and Brahminical archives simply left things out, and the historian's job is to supply the missing pages. We reject that framing. The archive is not incomplete. It is working exactly as designed.

A caste census that records who is untouchable is not neutral data collection; it is the administrative act that stabilises untouchability as a governable category. A crime statistic that logs only the cases police agree to register measures police discretion, not violence. To "fill the gap" is to accept the shape of the container.

So this collection is built as a double-entry ledger. On the left, we reproduce the state's arithmetic faithfully — case numbers, statutes, commission findings, the language of the FIR. On the right, we enter what that arithmetic was structured to discard. The two columns do not reconcile. That failure to reconcile is the finding.

The register · 1848 — present

ENTRIES,
DOUBLE-KEPT

Read left to right. The middle column is the entry as the record holds it. The right column is the entry as it is held elsewhere — in memory, in song, in a family's refusal to walk a certain road.

1848

Bhide Wada School, Pune

Savitribai and Jyotirao Phule open a school for Shudra and Ati-Shudra girls. Colonial education reports later fold it into general "native female education" statistics.

Education · Maharashtra

Savitribai carried a second sari to work. Dung and stones were thrown at her on the walk; she changed before class so the children would not see.

1924

Vaikom Satyagraha, Travancore

Twenty months of agitation over the right of avarna castes to use roads around the Vaikom Mahadeva temple. Recorded as a road-access dispute resolved by administrative order.

Mobility · Kerala

The dispute was never about a road. It was about whether a body could occupy space that a god had been made to own.

1927

Mahad Satyagraha · Chavdar Tank

20 March: Ambedkar leads thousands to drink from the public Chavdar tank. Caste Hindus "purify" the tank with cow dung, urine, milk and curd. On 25 December the Manusmriti is publicly burned.

Water · Maharashtra

The purification ritual is the confession. You do not cleanse water that was never made unclean. The tank testified against its own owners.

1932

The Poona Pact

24 September. Under Gandhi's fast-unto-death at Yerwada, Ambedkar surrenders the separate electorate granted to the Depressed Classes by the Communal Award, accepting reserved seats within a joint electorate.

Franchise · Constitutional

Filed in Indian textbooks as reconciliation. Ambedkar named it what it was: a signature obtained by holding a life hostage. Reserved seats gave Dalits representatives that caste-Hindu majorities could elect.

1936

Annihilation of Caste

Written as the presidential address to the Jat-Pat Todak Mandal, Lahore. The conference is cancelled rather than hear it. Ambedkar publishes it himself at his own cost.

Text · Undelivered

The most important speech in modern Indian political thought exists because the people who commissioned it refused to let it be spoken.

1950

Article 17, Constitution of India

"Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form forbidden; enforcement of any resulting disability is made a punishable offence.

Statute · Union

Seventy-six years later, the NCRB records twenty-four prosecutions in a year under the untouchability statute. The law abolished the word, not the arrangement.

1956

Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur

14 October. Ambedkar and an estimated 3–5 lakh followers convert to Buddhism, taking the twenty-two vows. He dies on 6 December, seven weeks later.

Conversion · Maharashtra

Conversion is filed by the state as a change of religious column. It was an act of exit — the refusal to be reformed inside a system whose terms were the problem.

1968

Kilvenmani, Thanjavur

25 December. Forty-four Dalit agricultural labourers — mostly women and children — are locked in a hut and burned alive during a wage dispute. The Madras High Court acquits all twenty-five convicted landlords in 1973.

Massacre · Tamil Nadu

The acquittal reasoning held that men of property were unlikely to have walked to the scene. Class standing was accepted as an alibi.

1972

Dalit Panthers, Bombay

Founded by Namdeo Dhasal, J.V. Pawar and Raja Dhale. A manifesto that refuses the term "Harijan" and defines Dalit to include workers, landless peasants, and women.

Movement · Maharashtra

Dhasal's Golpitha put the language of the brothel and the slum into Marathi literature. The literary establishment called it obscene. It was simply not translated into their vocabulary.

1989

SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act

Creates a specific category of "atrocity", special courts, and mandatory registration. Conviction rates under it remain persistently low; over three lakh cases sit pending.

Statute · Union

The Act's most common public appearance is as an allegation of misuse. A law written to be under-enforced is easiest to attack for over-enforcement.

1997

Laxmanpur-Bathe, Bihar

1 December. Fifty-eight Dalits killed by the Ranvir Sena. In 2013 the Patna High Court acquits all twenty-six accused convicted by the lower court.

Massacre · Bihar

Bathani Tola, 1996: twenty-one killed, all acquitted on appeal in 2012. The pattern is not failure of evidence. It is a judiciary that keeps arriving at the same answer.

2006

Khairlanji, Bhandara

29 September. Four members of the Bhotmange family — Surekha, Priyanka, Roshan, Sudhir — killed by a mob. Courts explicitly hold that caste was not a motive; PoA Act charges fail.

Massacre · Maharashtra

Surekha Bhotmange had refused to sell her land and had testified against neighbours. The court found no caste motive in the killing of a Dalit woman who would not yield land.

2016

Rohith Vemula · Una

17 January: Rohith Vemula, PhD scholar, dies by suicide at the University of Hyderabad after suspension and stipend withholding. 11 July: four Dalit men are stripped and flogged in Una, Gujarat, for skinning a dead cow — filmed by the attackers.

Institution · Cow politics

An official inquiry questioned whether Vemula was Dalit at all. The state's response to a caste death was to audit the caste of the dead.

2018

Bhima Koregaon

1 January. The bicentenary gathering at the Koregaon obelisk is attacked. The subsequent investigation pivots away from the attack and toward the arrest of academics, lawyers, poets and activists under anti-terror law.

Commemoration · Maharashtra

A Dalit victory over a Peshwa army, commemorated annually since Ambedkar's 1927 visit. The investigation converted the assaulted into the accused.

2023

Crime in India, NCRB

57,789 cases registered under crimes against Scheduled Castes. Uttar Pradesh 15,130; Rajasthan 8,449; Madhya Pradesh 8,232.

Data · National

A registered case requires a police station willing to register it. The map above is, in part, a map of where refusal to register is hardest to sustain.

The count

WHERE THE
REGISTER FILLS

Cases registered under crimes against Scheduled Castes, by state, 2023. Source: National Crime Records Bureau, Crime in India 2023. Read this as a map of registration, not a map of violence.

A caution the NCRB itself cannot print: the denominator is missing. These are cases the police agreed to register, in a system where the officer, the complainant and the accused all carry caste. Where atrocity is most normalised, registration is lowest. The states at the top of this chart are not necessarily the most violent — they are, among other things, the states where Dalit political organisation has made refusal to file an FIR most expensive.

Method & ethics

WHAT THIS
ARCHIVE REFUSES

Standing rules for Collection 04

1. No atrocity photography. This collection does not reproduce images of Dalit bodies in violation. The Una flogging was filmed by its perpetrators for circulation; to re-circulate it is to complete their act. We cite the record and describe the event. We do not supply the image.

2. Named victims, named perpetrators. Where courts have acquitted, we say so and we say who was acquitted. Anonymising the accused while naming the dead is the archive's oldest asymmetry.

3. State data is evidence, not truth. NCRB figures appear here as artefacts of a registration process, always with the process named.

4. No savarna ventriloquism. Where the counter-entry belongs to Dalit authorship, it is attributed to Dalit authorship. Where a line is the curator's own framing, it is marked as such.

5. Open to correction. This is a working file, not a monument. Corrections from the communities documented here take precedence over the curator's draft.

Global South Solidarities

A decolonial feminist digital humanities anarchive. The GSS Anarchive is not a repository of the past. It is an instrument for refusing the terms on which the past was filed.
