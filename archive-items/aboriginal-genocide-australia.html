§ 01 · The Argument

The genocide is not over. It is ongoing.

Polya argues that the genocide that began with the 1788 invasion continues today — not only in memory of what was, but in the concrete arithmetic of avoidable death in Indigenous communities right now.

The UN Genocide Convention names five categories of acts. Australia's history and present carry evidence of all five: killing, causing serious bodily and mental harm, deliberately inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about physical destruction, imposing measures to prevent births, and — most infamously — forcibly transferring children of the group.

This page presents the record as Polya compiles it. The figures are contested, and honestly named as his estimates. The larger truth they point to is not.

§ 03 · The Ongoing Toll

Four thousand avoidable deaths every year.

The genocide is not a chapter that closed. It is present-tense arithmetic.

0

avoidable Indigenous deaths per year — the difference between observed and expected mortality.

Polya breaks the figure down: an observed mortality rate of ~1.0% in the Indigenous population, against a non-Indigenous expected mortality of ~0.4%. The gap — 0.6% per year, applied to ~670,000 people — is the annual avoidable-death count.

Put another way: Indigenous Australians die at 1.9 times the rate of non-Indigenous Australians. Where wealthy-nation demographics predict a life, Australia counts a death.

§ 05 · The Long Killing

Frontier Wars, killing times, and after.

A chronology of the genocidal century-and-a-half — and of the settler policies that continued the work in a different key.

1788
Invasion. The Aboriginal Genocide begins.
The First Fleet arrives at Gadigal Country. A population Polya estimates at 350,000 to 1,000,000 lives across the continent, speaking 250 to 750 distinct languages.
1790
The Aboriginal Genocide commences in earnest.
Killing, poisoning, deliberate infection, and the destruction of Country begin the collapse. Polya calls this the formal start of the genocide.
1803 — 1876
Tasmanian Aboriginal Genocide.
The near-complete destruction of the Palawa/Pakana people, described in the historiography as “planned and intended by settlers”.
1800s — 1920s
The Frontier Wars — the “killing times.”
Named by Aboriginal oral history as the killing times. Massacres continued into the 1920s. Queensland's frontier is specifically documented as a genocide period.
early 1900s — 1970s
The Stolen Generations.
Roughly 100,000 Aboriginal children forcibly removed from their mothers under successive government policies. UN Convention Article II(e): forcibly transferring children of the group.
1967
Aboriginal Rights Referendum.
Indigenous Australians are counted in the census for the first time. It takes 179 years after invasion for the state to concede that Aboriginal people exist.
1975
Racial Discrimination Act passes.
Formal legal protections. Later, exemptions for the Northern Territory Intervention will suspend those protections for Indigenous communities.
2007
Australia refuses the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples.
Joining the US, Canada, and New Zealand. Reversed under the Rudd Labor Government in April 2009.
2000s — present
Northern Territory Intervention · community closures.
Race-based legislation exempts NT Aboriginal communities from the 1975 Act's protections. Policy plans to close up to 150 remote Aboriginal communities. Bilingual education is closed. A 90% English-language instruction mandate follows.

§ 07 · The Legal Standard

Every one of the five acts.

UN Convention on Genocide · Article II · The five acts
“…any of the following acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group…”
Killing members of the group.Frontier Wars, massacres, deaths in custody
Causing serious bodily or mental harm.Ongoing systemic violence, family separation trauma
Deliberately inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about physical destruction.Under-funded health, housing, education — the 4,000/yr avoidable-death arithmetic
Imposing measures intended to prevent births.Documented removals, sterilizations, and reproductive coercion
Forcibly transferring children of the group.The Stolen Generations · 100,000 children

§ 09 · What Was Said

The Convention requires intent to be established, in whole or in part. Polya's contention is that this record, together with the sustained pattern of settler and state action across two centuries, meets the standard many times over.
